# Moon Mars Memory Museum

Build a complete, modern, responsive educational website called SPACE LEGACY.



Project Concept:

SPACE LEGACY is an interactive digital museum that documents human exploration of the Moon and Mars. It focuses on the spacecraft, rovers, landers, and scientific equipment that remain on these worlds after their missions.



The goal is to help students and visitors understand the history, purpose, scientific achievements, and legacy of these machines through authentic information, interactive experiences, and visual storytelling.



1. Visual Direction and Design



Create a sophisticated science museum aesthetic inspired by NASA mission archives, aerospace research centers, and editorial documentary websites.



The design should feel carefully art-directed and human-designed, not like a generic AI-generated website.



Use:



- Deep charcoal and near-black backgrounds.

- Warm off-white text.

- Muted orange and Mars-red accents.

- Subtle gray borders and restrained highlights.

- Clean typography with a strong visual hierarchy.

- Generous whitespace and balanced layouts.

- High-quality real space photography.

- Subtle animations only when they improve the experience.



Avoid:



- Excessive purple and blue gradients.

- Glowing neon effects everywhere.

- Too many rounded cards.

- Generic AI dashboard layouts.

- Unnecessary animations.

- Fake statistics and invented mission information.

- Random decorative space icons.



2. Homepage



Create an immersive homepage with:



Navigation Bar



- SPACE LEGACY logo.

- Home.

- Explore Moon.

- Explore Mars.

- Missions.

- 3D Explorer.

- About.



Hero Section



Main headline:

"THE MACHINES WE LEFT BEHIND."



Supporting text:

"Explore the legacy of human exploration across the Moon and Mars. Discover the machines that went farther than we could, and the stories they left behind."



Add two clear navigation links:



- Explore the Moon

- Explore Mars



Use a large, authentic NASA space photograph as the hero background. Keep text readable and the layout elegant.



Featured Missions



Introduce selected machines:



- Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle.

- Sojourner Mars Rover.

- Opportunity Mars Rover.

- Spirit Mars Rover.

- InSight Mars Lander.



Each item should have an image, name, mission dates, and a short description.



Why They Matter



Create an editorial section explaining why these machines were sent, what they discovered, and why some remain on the Moon or Mars.



Explore the Museum



Provide a clear visual path to explore the Moon, Mars, and the 3D collection.



3. Equipment Pages



Create a reusable structure for every machine.



Each equipment page should include:



- Official name.

- Mission name.

- Launch and landing dates.

- Mission duration.

- Original NASA photographs.

- Historical background.

- Scientific purpose.

- Major achievements.

- Mission timeline.

- Interesting facts.

- 3D model viewer placeholder.



Do not invent scientific facts. Use clearly marked placeholders wherever verified information is not yet available.



4. Interactive Features



Prepare the website structure for:



- Interactive Moon and Mars maps.

- 3D models with rotation and zoom.

- Clickable machine components.

- Mission timelines.

- Educational quizzes.

- An AI Space Historian assistant.



These features can be implemented progressively. Do not create fake working interactions.



5. Technical Requirements



- Use React and TypeScript.

- Use clean, reusable components.

- Use responsive layouts for mobile, tablet, and desktop.

- Organize the project into maintainable components and pages.

- Use accessible navigation and readable typography.

- Keep performance in mind.

- Prepare integration points for official NASA APIs and future 3D models.

- Do not expose API keys in frontend code.



6. Content and Authenticity



Use NASA as the primary reference for mission information and photography.



Do not use AI-generated images as substitutes for historical NASA mission photographs.



Keep image sources and credits accessible.



Clearly distinguish historical facts from educational storytelling.



Final Design Goal



The final result should look like a carefully designed digital museum created by a passionate space exploration team.



It should be visually memorable, educational, trustworthy, easy to navigate, and suitable for presentation in the NASA Space Apps Challenge.



Start by building the complete homepage and the initial navigation structure. Focus on design quality and consistency before implementing advanced features.
